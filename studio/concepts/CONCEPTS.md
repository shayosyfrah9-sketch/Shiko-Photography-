# SHIKO WORLD 2.0: three concepts
Creative director, 2026-11-24. Concept phase only: nothing here is built. No images exist yet; every visual described below is a **plan** for a Rendered (Blender/Cycles) frame or a Mock-up, and will be labelled as one.

## What the concepts grew out of (verified by looking at his photos)
Contact sheets of festival, sport and portrait photos in `media/photos` show the same habits again and again:
- **Low sun from behind.** Golden-hour backlight through pines, sun flare, hair rim-lit (Shira, Maya). The beach portraits face west, with the Tel Aviv towers behind.
- **Suspended matter.** Water spray frozen in the air at FreeEarth (2w2a1130), surf spray at 1/2000 s (surf0001–0005), dust in a forest clearing.
- **Shallow depth of field.** The subject is sharp and the world dissolves into bokeh: festival tapestries, stadium stands.
- **Colour that is real, not graded in.** Maccabi yellow under floodlights at night, turquoise water at surf breaks, saturated stage fabrics, an orange sunset with the dancers as silhouettes.
- **He shoots from different distances.** Portraits are close (85 mm), festivals are inside the crowd, football is from the touchline with a telephoto lens, surfing is from the beach with a long lens.

So the three survivors are each built on one of three photographic truths: **light (when)**, **focus (what)** and **vantage (where)**. Shay's "decisive moment" needs all three. Each concept turns one of them into the whole world.

The 18 rooms that must survive are 7 festivals (FreeEarth, Ozora, Zoodana, Goaland, Bardak, Hippietipi, Sounds of Love), Football (Maccabi TA), Basketball (Maccabi Rishon, Reservists League), Surfing, 6 portrait sets (Mayu, Maya, Amit, Shira, Eden, Noa) and Video. Each room keeps its original photo order and gallery link.

## Killed ideas (brainstormed 11, kept 3)
| Idea | Why it died |
|---|---|
| Floating islands / Floating World (VISION A) | The most common "3D portfolio" image there is, and the islands read as game levels. The idea is a setting, not a metaphor. |
| Glass pavilion on a cliff (Aperture Pavilion experiment) | The critic already called it a cliché (3.0→5.4). Detailing cannot rescue a concept that has no idea in it. |
| Museum corridor / white-cube gallery walk | The "virtual exhibition" template. It makes the photos into wall decoration and makes the visitor walk. |
| World Beyond the Lens / step through the lens (VISION C) | A portal trope: the lens tunnel, then a reveal. It becomes a 5-second gimmick and then the site is an ordinary menu. |
| Cinematic Valley (VISION B) | A valley with four buildings is the current alpine village with a bigger budget. It is not "clearly beyond" v153. (Its cinematic ambition is absorbed into Concept A.) |
| Darkroom (prints developing in red light) | Film nostalgia. Shay shoots digital, and red safelight is exactly the "colour takeover" he dislikes. |
| Inside the camera body (mode dial = menu) | A product skeuomorph. It turns into a game hub with a dial. |
| Contact sheet / light table city | A grid, which is a template by definition. It gives no cinematic experience. |
| Darkness + flash reveals the world | The flashlight-horror game mechanic. It also hides the photos in darkness. |
| Walk into his photo (2.5D photo-to-3D) | It distorts and crops his real frames. The photo stops being the art and becomes a texture. |
| Chronophotography burst (Muybridge strip you scroll through) | A known art trope, and it duplicates A's linear scrub. Its best part (frozen motion) moved into Concept C. |

## A. SUNLINE: one day of light
**Core idea:** The site is one continuous day on the Mediterranean coast, from first surf to last floodlight. You do not travel through rooms, you travel through hours, because a decisive moment starts with the decision of *when*: what the light is doing.

**Experience**
- **First 6 s.** Black, then a single still of the coast at **the real current hour in Israel** (from research idea 5, "Israel-time light"), with the white Shiko camera mark small and low. At night the stadium is already lit; at 18:00 the beach is golden. At 1.5 s the light starts to move (a real time-lapse) and the camera drifts along the waterline. At 4 s the sun-arc appears at the top of the screen (a thin hairline arc with 5 icon marks), together with the WhatsApp and booking icons. Any input skips straight to the main world.
- **Main world (Home) = the time-slice.** One high oblique shot of the whole coast in which each stretch of the coast is lit by its own hour: dawn surf in the near south, noon festival fields, a golden kurkar cliff, a blue-hour stadium, and midnight at the far north end. This is the real photographic "time-slice" technique, used as the map. Tap a stretch to go there. Home (one tap, top-left camera icon) always returns here.
- **Entering a field.** The camera time-lapses there (sky racing, shadows swinging) in ≤ 1.6 s, then drops from the rail (~30 m) to eye height and settles.
- **Viewing photos.** At the room's minute, time pauses. The photo rises front-parallel on a thin ivory mount at its true aspect ratio, and the paused 3D hour stays behind it, dimmed to 35% and softly blurred. Swipe or ←→ moves through the photos in their original order. The gallery link sits under the counter.
- **Booking / WhatsApp.** These are in the dock at all times, one tap. Booking opens with one poetic but useful question: "Which light?" (dawn / day / golden / night), and then the normal form.

**The four fields on the clock** (the order of rooms is kept; the minutes are poetic)
- 06:10 **Surfing**: the morning sun from the land front-lights the waves, as in his surf shots.
- 11:00 → 19:40 **Festivals**: seven canopies along a dune meadow, one per successive hour. The light runs from FreeEarth's midday spray to Sounds of Love's orange silhouettes.
- 18:30 **Portraits & Branding**: the pine edge and the beach in backlight, six spots along the sand.
- 20:15 **Sport**: the stadium bowl switches on (Maccabi yellow), then the basketball hall glows (Rishon, Reservists).
- 00:00 **Video**: at midnight the whole day hangs as **one long-exposure still**: every frame of the day blended into a single image, after Sugimoto's *Theaters* (research idea 2). Touch it and the day replays as a 12 s time-lapse that cuts into the showreel. Stills are moments and video is time, so video lives where time is replayed. Only one video ever decodes at a time.

**Camera & navigation.** Desktop: the wheel, a horizontal drag or ←→ moves time forward or back with inertia, snapping magnetically to each room's minute. Icons on the sun-arc jump straight to a field. Phone: a vertical swipe moves time (it feels like a scroll), a tap on the arc jumps, and a horizontal swipe browses photos. In Hebrew the arc and the time direction run right to left. Reduced motion: no flight, only 400 ms cross-fades between stills of each hour.

**Art direction.** Palette: the day's own light, with no grading on top. Blue dawn #2b3a4f, noon white sand, golden #e9a25a, blue hour #1d2433, floodlight 5600K. Materials: wet sand, kurkar sandstone, pine, canvas canopies, concrete. Atmosphere: sea haze that thickens toward evening. Sound: the soundscape follows the clock (gulls → distant bass → cicadas → crowd → silence), silent until the first gesture, with one mute control.

**Photos.** The photos are never placed inside the 3D world as textures. They always appear on the neutral mount at their true ratio, so the day's tint never touches their colour.

**Performance.** The world is **pre-rendered Cycles footage scrubbed by input** (seek-friendly H.264 with a 6-frame GOP). Nine segments are each streamed when the visitor approaches them. Desktop 1600×900, ~1.8 MB per segment. Phone: a separately composed vertical render at 720×1280, ~0.9 MB per segment. The time-slice Home is a single still (AVIF, 220 KB desktop / 110 KB phone), so the first meaningful paint is < 1.5 s on 4G. Real time is limited to a low-poly proxy of the coast plus the exported Blender camera (the Lusion pattern from the research, §1.2/§2.5). The proxy carries the photo planes, a live sun glint and ±1.5° of look-around. The proxy is synced to the footage with `requestVideoFrameCallback`. It runs on WebGL2 and stays well under the 80-draw-call phone target. The result is the same quality on every device, with no GPU dependency.

**Not a template or a game.** There is no avatar and no "level". The single control is the sun. The map is a real photographic technique, not a minimap.

**Visualisation plan**
1. *Key frame 1, Home time-slice (Rendered + composite).* The coast is a 3 km S-curve running north. Camera at (−400, −900, 420) m, 28 mm, pitch −22°, looking north. Six renders of the same camera (sun at −4°, 15°, 60°, 6°, −3° and −12° with the floodlights on) are blended with soft masks along the coast. Composition: the shoreline is the leading line from bottom-left to upper-right; the stadium crown glows on the upper third; the night sky is at the top right.
2. *Key frame 2, Golden hour portraits (Rendered).* Camera at 1.5 m height on the sand, 85 mm, sun at 4° altitude behind a pine edge in frame left (backlight, natural flare). Kurkar cliff and sand ripples are in the foreground. Far up the coast, in the right third, the stadium floodlights are just switching on, so the next hour is waiting.
3. *Photo viewing (Mock-up).* Shira 2w2a0443 (horizontal, backlit) on an ivory mount at 70% of the viewport width over frame 2 dimmed and blurred. The arc shows 18:42; ‹ › 3/24; gallery, WhatsApp.

**Risks.** Scroll-scrubbed video is a known Awwwards trope and must be lifted by the time-slice idea. CPU-only Cycles cost is high: ~1,300 frames at ~3 min each is ~65 h, so lower samples + OIDN and reuse between segments are needed. The visitor cannot look around freely (pre-rendered). Strong day-colour shifts could feel like the "colour takeovers" Shay dislikes. The coast is compressed, so it is not geographically real.

## B. DEPTH OF FIELD: the focus ring is the map
**Core idea:** One view that never moves. You choose what to see by turning the focus. Each field of his work lives at the distance from which he actually shoots it, because a decisive moment is also a decision about *what* is sharp.

**Experience**
- **First 6 s.** The screen opens as pure bokeh: big soft discs of floodlights, string lights and the last sun on the sea, with the camera mark in ivory. This is not a loading screen: the blur *is* the loading strategy, while the sharp layers stream in. From 2 to 4.5 s the focus rolls from ∞ to near, and the sea, a stadium, a festival meadow and a pine ledge each snap into sharpness in turn. At 4.5 s the focus settles at the *hyperfocal* distance (everything acceptably sharp) and a lens-style distance scale fades in along the bottom edge as the navigation. Any input ends the intro.
- **The world.** A single photograph Shay never took: twilight from a pine ridge of kurkar sandstone above the coast, looking west.
  - 1.5 m: the ledge and an empty portrait set (a linen backdrop on a stand, waiting).
  - 12 m: the festival meadow below, with seven patterned canopies under string lights.
  - 120 m: a stadium crown with four floodlight masts and the glowing roof of a hall.
  - ∞: the sea, a surf line and the last apricot band of light.
- **Home = hyperfocal.** One tap on the camera icon returns from any state to the sharp overview.
- **Entering a field.** You focus on it. The camera does not travel: the plane sharpens, everything else melts, and ambient sound follows the focus (cicadas near, muffled bass at 12 m, crowd at 120 m, surf at ∞). The rooms in that plane appear as small true-ratio prints standing in it. In the festival plane, for example, the seven canopies each get a cover print, in order left to right, mirrored for Hebrew.
- **Viewing photos.** Tap a print and it comes forward, flat, on an ivory mount, while the world behind drops to f/1.2 with nothing in focus. The world becomes the bokeh of his photo, which is how his own portraits look. The bokeh discs lean 10–15% toward that photo's own palette over about 600 ms (research idea 3, "the world takes its light from the photograph"). The world tints toward the photo; it never tints the photo. ←→ or a swipe moves through the photos, and a swipe down returns to the plane.
- **Video lives past ∞.** Turn the ring past infinity, as a real lens allows. The still vista begins to *move* (pines breathe, canopies ripple, surf rolls, floodlights flicker) and dissolves into the showreel.
- **Booking / WhatsApp.** Top right, two icons, always visible.

**The four fields by distance.** Portraits & Branding at 1.5 m (the intimate distance), Festivals at 12 m (inside the crowd), Sport at 120 m (football and basketball, telephoto), Surfing at ∞ (the long lens from the beach), Video past ∞.

**Camera & navigation.** The camera is fixed, with three real lens behaviours: focus *breathing* (~2% magnification change), ±0.3° handheld drift, and ±1.5% parallax from the mouse or gyro. Desktop: the wheel is the focus ring, with detents at the marks; ↑↓ or 1–5 also work; hover shows *focus peaking* (a faint ivory edge-shimmer on what would become sharp). Phone: **tap anywhere to focus there**, the gesture everyone already uses in their phone camera; a vertical swipe is fine focus. Reduced motion: no blur animation, a 300 ms cross-fade between five pre-composed focus states, gyro off.

**Art direction.** The "blue-gold" minute: the sun is 2° below the sea horizon (the coast faces west, so this is correct). Sky indigo #1d2433 to apricot #e9a25a. Warm string lights (2700K), cool floodlights (5600K), kurkar ochre, dark olive pine. Atmosphere: layered coastal haze, which is essential so that the depth reads between the planes. Materials: linen, sandstone, bark, canvas, concrete, water. Sound: the ambience is mixed by focus, so focus is audio too.

**Performance (the lightest of the three).** Six RGBA depth layers rendered in Cycles: desktop 2560×1440, plus a separate vertical composition for phones at 1170×2532. Blur is done by mixing a pre-blurred mip stack in a tiny WebGL shader, which is cheap enough for 60 fps on mid-range phones and has a Canvas2D fallback. Budget: desktop ≈ 1.8 MB, phone ≈ 0.9 MB, under the research's 2.5 MB first-view target, with about 10 draw calls, plus the lazy Video plate (6 s loop, 720p, 1.5 MB). First paint is a 40 KB blurred frame, which is exactly the intro image.

**Not a template or a game.** There is no movement through space, no HUD and no avatar. The interface is a lens distance scale, and the one interaction is a real photographic act. The whole site is composed like a single photograph.

**Visualisation plan**
1. *Key frame 1, Hyperfocal Home (Rendered).* Camera at (0, 0, 1.55) m on the ridge, facing west downhill, 50 mm, pitch −6°, everything sharp. Pine bough in the upper-left corner. Portrait set in the right third at 1.5 m. Canopies across the lower-middle band at 10–30 m. Stadium crown left of centre at ~300 m. Sea horizon on the upper-third line. Sun at −2°.
2. *Key frame 2, Focus at the festivals (Rendered, real DOF f/1.4 at 12 m).* Same camera. The canopies and string lights are razor sharp. The ledge melts into dark olive blur. The floodlights become huge soft discs floating over the sea. This frame sells "bokeh as world".
3. *Photo viewing (Mock-up).* FreeEarth 2w2a1130 (vertical, the spray dancer) at 64% of the viewport height on an ivory mount, over frame 2 re-rendered with focus at 0.3 m (pure bokeh). Hairline ‹ › 1/30, gallery link, WhatsApp.

**Risks.** 2.5D layers can look like cardboard cut-outs if the parallax is pushed. Stadium, festival and sea in one believable view needs careful compression and haze. It may feel "small" because there is no journey, and Shay asked for a *world*. 18 rooms in one frame risks crowding. The phone needs its own composition. Non-photographers may not know "focus" (tap-to-focus mitigates this).

## C. WHERE HE STOOD: the held moment
**Core idea:** The world is one held instant: spray, dust, confetti and ticker-tape hang motionless in the air, but **the people are missing**. You find the exact spot where Shay stood, and at that spot his photograph puts the person back into the moment. The decisive moment is being in the right place when time stops.

**Experience**
- **First 6 s.** Silence, then one shutter click. A sunset tidal flat, wet sand like a mirror. The camera glides low across it, and you slowly realise that nothing moves: a wave stopped mid-break, a cloud of water drops hanging gold in the backlight. At 5 s the camera settles on the overview, and WhatsApp, booking and the four field icons fade in. Any input skips.
- **Main world (Home).** A wide, shallow tidal flat 3 minutes before sunset, with four held moments standing on it, each doubled by its reflection:
  - **Surf:** a frozen wave lip with a spray curtain at the sea edge.
  - **Festivals:** a stretched tapestry canopy with water spray and paper confetti suspended beneath it.
  - **Sport:** a cloud of Maccabi-yellow ticker-tape in floodlight beams over a patch of turf, with a ball hanging in it; and a hall doorway where an orange ball hangs above a rim.
  - **Portraits & Branding:** a pine grove edge where backlit dust and a linen sheet lifted by the wind hang in the air.

  Home (one tap) always rises back to this overview.
- **Entering a field.** Where he stood is marked diegetically: a pair of footprints in the wet sand, revealed by raking light, with no glowing markers. Tap a field: the camera walks there in 1.2 s and settles at Shay's eye height **with Shay's lens** (85 mm for portraits, 200 mm for surf). Each of the 18 rooms is one vantage around its held moment (the 7 festivals stand in a ring around the canopy, in order), so moving between rooms means circling the same instant.
- **Viewing photos: "the click".** When the camera arrives, the frozen matter lines up, a shutter sounds, and the room's cover photo appears exactly registered in the frame line, at its true aspect ratio and uncropped, as a window cut into the held moment. The real spray in the photo continues the 3D spray around it, and for one second the real ambience (crowd roar, surf, bass) bursts in, then silence again. Tap: the photo flattens to the front-parallel viewer (ivory mount, world dimmed), with the full set in its original order behind ←→. A swipe up turns the print over to **the back of the print**, after Bo Bardi's labels on the back of the glass (research idea 1). The back gives one line in Shay's words, the place, the time and the shutter speed from EXIF: why he stood here.
- **Video = time resumes.** At the Video vantage, pressing lets time run for 5 s: the wave breaks, confetti falls, ticker-tape drifts. Then it cuts into the showreel, and when you return, the world is held again.
- **Booking = your empty vantage.** A fifth set of footprints faces a held moment with no photo yet ("your moment"), and it opens booking and WhatsApp. They are also in the dock at all times, one tap.

**Camera & navigation.** This is the only concept with real-time 3D movement, but it is **choose-and-glide, never steer-and-walk**. The research found that an Awwwards jury marked a "playable world" portfolio down to 6.81 on usability (research §1.6). Desktop: click footprints or icons; ←→ circles the vantages of a field, ↑↓ moves between fields; drag to look around ±25° yaw and ±10° pitch; the wheel dollies back toward the overview. Phone: a horizontal swipe arcs around the held moment, a tap takes the shot (enters), and the gyro gives look-around. Reduced motion: cut-dissolves between vantages, no camera travel and no audio bursts.

**Art direction.** Sun at 2.5°, always behind the held matter, so every drop is a rim-lit gold bead (his own backlight habit). Palette: wet slate #3b4650, apricot sky #f0b27a, ivory spray, deep teal sea. The matter colours come from his photos and are kept restrained: festival saffron, magenta and teal, Maccabi yellow, basketball orange. Materials: wet sand, water (real refraction in the renders), paper, linen, turf. Sound: almost nothing (time stopped) except soft footsteps in wet sand, then one burst of life at each click.

**Performance (the heaviest of the three).**
- **Renderer:** three.js r181 **WebGLRenderer (WebGL2)**, not WebGPU, for v1 (research §2.1).
- **Lighting and sky:** baked KTX2 lightmaps (ETC1S) on the flat; a sky from a KTX2 HDR.
- **Suspended matter:** GPU-instanced impostors, 80k on desktop and 15k on phone, with depth-sorted soft sprites for the spray.
- **Phone budget:** ≤ 80 draw calls, ≤ 300K triangles, DPR cap 1.5. The first view is ≤ 2.5 MB (overview + nearest field); the other fields stream later. Desktop total ≤ 6 MB gz.
- **First paint:** a pre-rendered poster of the exact first frame (≤ 150 KB), so the hand-off to real time is invisible.
- **Phone strategy (research §2.5):** pre-rendered Cycles backplates per vantage + a light 3D proxy carrying only the matter nearest the camera and the photo plane, with 1.2 s flight clips (≤ 0.4 MB each) between vantages. Full real time only where the device tier allows it.

**Not a template or a game.** No avatar, no collectibles, no HUD. There are no people in the world: the only humans are in his photographs, so the photos are not decoration but the missing piece. It is a pause, not a level.

**Visualisation plan**
1. *Key frame 1, Overview (Rendered).* Camera at (0, −60, 14) m, 35 mm, pitch −12°, facing ~20° off the sun so all the matter is backlit. Frozen wave lip and glowing spray curtain in the right third. Festival canopy with hanging confetti in the left third at ~80 m. Ticker-tape cloud in floodlight beams far centre. Footprints as a leading line from the bottom-left. Reflections double everything.
2. *Key frame 2, the surf vantage before the click (Rendered).* Camera at 1.2 m above the sand, 200 mm, looking at the wave lip 70 m out. The spray curtain is frozen with a **surfer-shaped absence** in it, where the board cut through, and sun flare behind. The moment the photo is missing.
3. *Photo viewing (Mock-up).* Surf0001 registered into that absence, its spray continuous with the 3D spray, then a second panel of the flattened viewer: true ratio, ivory mount, 1/7, gallery, WhatsApp.

**Risks.** Bullet-time and "WebGL particle field" are both known looks, and the matter must read as physical, never as particles. Registering 18 vantages to 18 photos takes hand art direction per photo, and only the cover photo registers. A surreal tidal flat could drift into Dalí or Monument Valley dreaminess. A world without people could feel eerie. It is the heaviest on phones and the hardest to make fast.

## How the three differ
| | A. SUNLINE | B. DEPTH OF FIELD | C. WHERE HE STOOD |
|---|---|---|---|
| Photographic truth | Light (when) | Focus (what) | Vantage (where) |
| Spatial structure | Linear: one coastline = one day | A single fixed view, layered in depth | Open plain, constellation of 18 vantages |
| Navigation model | Scrub time (1 axis) | Turn focus / tap-to-focus, camera never moves | Hop between vantages, circle a frozen instant |
| Home | Time-slice of the whole day | Hyperfocal (all sharp) | Overview of the held flat |
| Video | Midnight replay of the day | Focus past ∞, the vista comes alive | Time resumes for 5 s |
| Tech | Pre-rendered Cycles footage, scrubbed | 6 baked depth layers + shader blur | Real-time 3D + baked light, video fallback |
| Weight (phone) | ~0.9 MB per segment, streamed | ≈ 0.9 MB total | ≤ 3 MB |

**Shared on purpose:**
- Photos are always shown front-parallel, at true ratio, uncropped, on a thin ivory mount.
- Home, WhatsApp and booking are one tap away.
- Icons, not text, in the header; RTL/LTR; reduced-motion variants; silent until a gesture.

**Shared technical rules, adopted from `studio/research/world2-research.md`:**
- WebGL2 for v1.
- Phone first view ≤ 2.5 MB and ≤ 80 draw calls.
- Photos as AVIF/JPEG (never ETC1S), 1280–1600 px inside 3D and full resolution only in the HTML lightbox.
- ≤ 8 photo textures resident on a phone, disposed on room exit.
- View Transitions API for the HTML layer (gallery → lightbox → booking).
- Real-device timing before any performance claim.

**Verified vs assumed.** Verified: room list and order (index.html), photo habits (contact sheets of 27 photos), tool limits (TOOLS.md: Cycles CPU only, no EEVEE). Assumed: all file-size and render-time budgets are estimates, not measurements; the focal lengths per field are inferred from the look of the photos, not from EXIF.
